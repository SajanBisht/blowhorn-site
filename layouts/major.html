The Blowhard incident

Young Private Blowhorn was told to whisper "lights out" to one barracks. He leaned into the camp megaphone, whispered, and every barracks in four counties went to bed. So did a weather balloon.

The general promoted him to Major on the spot, mostly so he would stop being a private problem. By supper the troops had a new name for him. Major Blowhard.

Nobody says it to his face. He always hears. He has very good ears. That is the whole job.

Old Thunderlung

His horn is a dented brass trumpet with a red tassel and one valve that sticks. He found it in the henhouse rafters on his first day, already dented, already loud.

Its first note made the sun come up twenty minutes early. Nobody has proved otherwise. He polishes it every morning and tells it the plan, because a horn should know where it is going.

He will not let anyone else play it. "Is not instrument," he says. "Is colleague."

Top crow

Every yard has one top crow. Not the loudest bird. The first one up, the one who makes sure everybody hears the day has started.

That is how he sees your launch. One message out, many ears in. He does not care who is loudest. He cares who answers.
