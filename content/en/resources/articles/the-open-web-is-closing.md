Reddit just reported a strong Q3 2026: revenue of $895 million, up 53% year over year. Its stock fell 30% anyway. As a [venture investor's widely shared analysis](https://lnkd.in/p/gX4awBSH) explains, the market was reacting to a disclosure buried in the results: search referral traffic declined during the quarter and is now falling by more than a million referrals a week.

## What happened

Reddit's CEO blames Google. Google's AI products use Reddit's content to answer the question, so the reader never needs to visit Reddit.

Reddit's response is to close up. It plans to put much more of the site behind a login and push more people to its app, trading reach for engagement. Logged-in and app users already bring in more than 80% of its revenue, so the trade makes sense for Reddit. If other platforms follow, the open web moves from "crawl freely and earn from referrals" to "restrict access, license the data and own the user relationship".

## What it means if you build an audience in public

**Search traffic is a weaker bet.** If one of the most distinctive collections of writing on the internet cannot hold on to its referrals, a company blog or landing page that relies on free search traffic should not count on keeping its share.

**People check AI against people.** The same analysis says half of US shoppers use Reddit to verify AI recommendations. The answer may come from an AI summary, but trust still comes from first-person experience: opinions, context, disagreement. That is where your audience goes to decide.

**A real voice is the scarce thing.** As synthetic content becomes abundant, a real person who shows up consistently stands out more, not less. One commenter on that analysis complains that bots have taken over Reddit and AI slop gets posted all over it. That is the bar: be the opposite of it.

**Logins keep out crawlers, not members.** A logged-in Reddit is built to shut out scrapers. A member who signs in and takes part is the user Reddit wants. Platforms may also tighten their bot detection, which is one more reason anything you automate should behave like you.

## How to show up where your audience is

1. **Post natively.** Make the post itself worth reading on the platform, not just a link back to your site.
2. **Join before you post.** On Reddit, comment helpfully in a community before you share anything of yours, and keep promotion to a sliver. The [Reddit briefing](/field-manual/reddit/) quotes the rules.
3. **Write for each community.** A post written for one subreddit is not a post for the next one, and the same comment twice is spam. Reddit names generative AI tools in its spam rules ([Reddit briefing](/field-manual/reddit/#what-gets-you-removed-or-banned)).
4. **Keep a human pace.** Spread posts out and post when the community is awake. A burst of posts can look like a bot.
5. **Be there to answer.** A reply from the author is what turns a post into a conversation.
