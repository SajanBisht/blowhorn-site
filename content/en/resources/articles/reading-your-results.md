A finished activity tells a simple story on the surface: the post went out. Underneath, the numbers on that activity are the feedback loop for everything you schedule next. Reading them well takes five minutes a week, and it beats any amount of guessing about what your audience wants.

## Start with the outcome, not the count

Every activity ends in one of three states: posted, posted with warnings, or failed. The count of posts is the least interesting number on the page. What matters is the pattern across a run of activities:

- **Posted cleanly, repeatedly.** The troop, the platform and the pace are all healthy. Your only job is to not fiddle with it.
- **Posted, but slowly.** The work landed, but the run took much longer than the same troop used to. That is an early signal: a platform quietly tightening its limits, or a queue building up behind a few slow posts.
- **Failed with a reason.** A failure in Blowhorn always carries its reason, and the reason is the product. "Target gone", "platform refused", and "not signed in" ask for three completely different fixes. Treat the reason as the to-do, not the red mark as the problem.

## The three numbers worth watching

**Success rate by troop.** One troop dipping while the others hold steady points at that troop's target list or content, not at your account. Everything dipping at once points the other way: a platform change, or a sign-in that needs attention.

**Time from scheduled to posted.** A healthy schedule posts close to its planned time. A growing gap means your queue is longer than your pace allows, which usually means the fix is spacing, not speed. The scheduling craft is covered in the next article in this series.

**Replies and clicks, where the platform reports them.** These are the only numbers that describe the audience rather than the machinery. A post can be a mechanical success and a content failure. If the machinery is green and the response is flat for two weeks running, the schedule is fine and the material is the problem.

## Change one thing at a time

The most common way to learn nothing from your results is to change the content, the timing and the troop in the same week. When the numbers move, you will not know which change moved them.

Work like this instead:

1. Pick the weakest signal from the list above.
2. Make the smallest change that addresses it: one troop's spacing, one platform's list, one post rewritten.
3. Let it run for a full cycle of that troop before judging.
4. Write down what you changed, in the activity notes or your own log. Future you is the audience for that note.

## What "good" looks like

Good is not a number, it is a trend you can explain. A steady success rate you trust, posting times that land when you planned them, and failures you can name the cause of before you open the activity. When all three hold, your posting runs itself, and your five minutes a week goes back to the content, which is where it belonged all along.
