Every platform you post to reserves the right to change the rules, and they exercise it: a daily limit drops, a link format stops working, a kind of post that was fine last month now needs a different flow. Nothing about your schedule changed. Everything about its results can.

## How a rule change shows up

Rule changes rarely arrive with an announcement you will see. They arrive as a pattern in your activity:

- **A new failure reason, repeated.** One failure is an accident; the same new reason across a troop is a policy. Read the reason text carefully - platforms usually say what changed, in their own compressed way.
- **A success rate that steps down, not drifts.** Content problems erode slowly. A cliff edge, where a troop goes from fine on Tuesday to failing on Wednesday, is a rule change until proven otherwise.
- **Runs that slow down.** A platform enforcing a tighter limit often does it by delaying rather than refusing. Posts land, but later and later. That is a limit introducing itself.

## What Blowhorn does about it

Blowhorn posts from your own browser, signed in as you, so it lives under the same limits you do - and it treats those limits as information:

- Posting runs watch for the refusal and slow-down signals above, and the failure reason lands on the activity, in plain language, not as a bare error code.
- Pace controls - typing delay, concurrency and approval gates - are the adjustment dials. When a platform tightens, the response is a slower, smaller, more deliberate run, not a retry storm.
- A failed post is never retried blindly in a loop. The cause is shown first, because on a changed platform the old retry is how accounts get from "limited" to "suspended".

## Your three moves

1. **Pause the affected troop, not everything.** Other platforms and other troops are almost always unaffected. A platform-specific pattern gets a platform-specific response.
2. **Shrink and slow the next run.** Halve the batch, widen the spacing, and turn on approval for the first run so you watch each post land before trusting the troop again.
3. **Re-check before you restore.** Run the smaller shape for a full cycle. If it posts clean, step the pace back up gradually rather than returning to the old settings in one jump.

## The mindset

A rule change is not an attack on your schedule; it is the platform repricing attention. The accounts that come through changes cleanly are the ones whose activity already looked human - spaced, varied, and modest in volume - because they have the least to change when the rules move.
